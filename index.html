      How the system works

      The platform is one stage of a loop, not the whole of it. The loop is drawn below, and each stage says whether it is built or planned.

      1. Gaps are collected

        Published reviews and guidelines naming data that is missing about women are found, checked against the live web to see whether it has been collected since, and added to the platform once a person has confirmed the claim against its source.

        Seven steps. A record passes all seven before it reaches the seed, and the last one is a person.

        1. Discover

          Europe PMC is searched for the sentences a systematic review writes when it looked and found nothing — “no studies met the inclusion criteria”, “insufficient evidence to determine” — restricted to reviews and guidelines, and to the last twelve months. Searching what an introduction says instead finds gaps the paper itself is about to close.

        2. Filter

          A cheap model answers one yes/no question per abstract: does this name missing data about women that the paper does not itself go on to collect?

        3. Extract

          Structured output strictly against the data.js schema. Anything the source text does not support comes back null, not a guess.

        4. Verify

          A live web search asks whether the data has been collected since the gap was named. Where it finds partial coverage the record becomes partial and names the dataset that exists; where it finds the data in full, the candidate is not a gap and never enters the register. The check is dated, and no search proves a negative, which every missing record says on its face.

        5. Design

          A possible study design for the data need, generated and kept out of the register. It is an answer, not a record: it never reaches data.js, and it is labelled AI-generated and unverified wherever it appears.

          A design is not offered for every record. Ordinary JavaScript rules in intake/validate.mjs refuse one that assigns an intervention to pregnant women, women in labour, children or a comparable group without naming its ethics approval, consent procedure and independent safety monitoring; one that doses a participant with something it does not name; or one that restricts eating without screening for eating disorders. Three of the designs generated so far were refused on those grounds.

          A refused design is never repaired by hand and never shown. The record keeps its claim, its date and its check, and says on its face that no design is offered — because a card labelled AI-generated may not carry a sentence a person wrote, and because withholding a design costs a convenience while publishing a harmful one costs the thing this platform exists to be.

        6. Validate

          Ordinary JavaScript, no model. A record whose claim does not quote its source word for word is rejected — the quotation is checked against the abstract it came from. So is guidance with no source, a record nobody checked, and a claim that data exists without saying where. Duplicates go by DOI.

        7. Review

          The run stops. Nothing reaches the seed until a person has decided on it. For the first six records the AI fetched every abstract and compared it against the claim, a person made the call on each one, and the AI merged the approved blocks — so the comparison was machine-made and the decision human. Three of the nine were dismissed, each a paper that collected the very data it called missing. Every decision and its reason is logged, and the paper is never offered again.

        Most tools point a model at a problem and let it generate the answer. This one inverts that. Where the data gap is, is a claim, and every claim here is borrowed from a published source and attributed. AI is used for finding, extracting and checking — never for making the claim. Nothing the model composes is stored as a record: a study design is generated when a researcher asks for one, labelled AI-generated, and kept out of the register entirely.

      2. The platform shows what is missing

        For each problem: which data is needed, what status it has, who said it was missing and when, and when the platform last checked whether it has been collected since.

      3. A researcher picks up a request

        The platform hands over a draft design — which women, which variables, which breakdowns — labelled AI-generated and unreviewed, and withheld where the rules refuse one. Reviewing that draft and running the study is the researcher's work, and none of that is built here.

      4. Women on the platform join the study

        Women who want the gap in their own problem closed take part in it.

      5. The collected data closes the gap

        And the record’s status changes. The loop returns to stage 2.

      Who built it, and how

      • Built by Marina, solo, for the Elevate Women Global Hackathon 2026.
      • Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. No framework, no build step, no dependencies — three screens and one seed file do not need one, and a build is one more thing that can fail on submission day. It opens by double-clicking index.html.
      • Nothing is invented and stored. A record carries what its source said and nothing more, and the validator rejects one that does not. A study design — which women, which variables, which breakdowns, in what form — is generated when a researcher asks for it, labelled AI-generated, and never becomes a record.
      • Disclosure. Everything in this repository was written during the Hackathon Period, 14–20 September 2026. No pre-existing code was used; an earlier personal project by the same author shares no code with this one. Development was AI-assisted throughout, using Claude Code; the git history is the record.
